# RE-003 Performance Review

## Decision

The baseline saturated-rejection path reproducibly used 3 current-thread allocations per rejected attempt (3,000 over 1,000 attempts). A private lazy-construction candidate reduced that measurement to 0 allocations while preserving the recovered callable. It was not retained: the candidate did not meet the required stable 5% throughput or p95 improvement in the 8/32-producer short-task cases, and the measurements showed material regressions in several other scenarios. Final production code remains at the baseline revision; the probe and this report are retained.

The candidate was tested only as one local implementation. Its constructor ran after lifecycle, task-ID, and capacity checks while holding the state mutex, which kept admission atomic but extended the critical section on accepted submissions. Results did not justify that tradeoff. This is a measured engineering decision, not a public performance guarantee.

The baseline itself varied substantially between independent runs (for example, 8-producer short-task median throughput differed by more than 2× between B1 and B2). This dispersion makes isolated apparent gains unreliable. Across the pooled 10 samples, candidate throughput medians were lower in all six scenarios; p95 medians were also higher in all six. The required 8/32-producer short-task improvement was therefore not demonstrated, and the candidate was reverted.

## Final RE-003 status

Saturated retry cost was measured and reproduced; a private allocation-avoidance candidate was tested and rejected by the design threshold. The final change adds the reusable measurement and its output-format test only. No production performance improvement is claimed.
